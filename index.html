Lesson 1 · 4 min · interactive

What is SMILES?

A molecule is a set of atoms joined by bonds — a graph. SMILES (Simplified Molecular Input Line Entry System) writes that graph as a short line of text, so it can be typed, stored in databases such as PubChem or ChEMBL, and read by programs.

The rules you need

  • C N O S P F Cl Br I — atoms; hydrogens are added automatically. CCO is ethanol.
  • = double bond, # triple bond. C=C ethene, C#N a nitrile.
  • ( ) a branch hanging off the previous atom: CC(C)C is isobutane.
  • 1 … 9 ring closure: the same digit twice closes a ring. C1CCCCC1 cyclohexane.
  • lowercase c n o — aromatic atoms: c1ccccc1 is benzene.
  • [ ] anything special: charges [NH3+], isotopes [13C], explicit H.
  • @ @@ handedness of a stereocentre; / \ cis/trans (E/Z) around a double bond.

Lesson 2 · 2 min

From text to 3D

SMILES says who is bonded to whom, not where the atoms are. But chemistry happens in 3D: how a drug fits a receptor, what a spectrum looks like, how a molecule reacts. A 3D structure (x, y, z for every atom) is the starting point for docking, molecular dynamics, quantum chemistry and many ML models.

MethodHow it builds 3DUse it when
RDKit (ETKDGv3)Distance geometry: random coordinates that satisfy distance bounds from the graph, refined with experimental torsion preferences.Default. Reliable stereochemistry, drug-like molecules.
OpenBabelBuilds from fragment templates, then a short force-field clean-up.Second opinion, unusual elements, cross-checking RDKit.
NetworkXA graph layout (Kamada-Kawai) with chemically sensible target distances; no force field.A quick sketch or teaching. Approximate for fused rings and macrocycles.
Automatic checks. ELAYA reads every 3D structure back and compares it with your SMILES (stereocentres, E/Z bonds, bond lengths). If something does not match, a warning appears above the coordinates; when everything is right, nothing is shown.
Lesson 3 · 3 min

Energy: force fields and neural potentials

Every arrangement of the atoms has an energy. Picture a landscape: valleys are stable shapes, hills are strained ones. Optimising a geometry means rolling downhill to the nearest valley. Energies in ELAYA are in kcal/mol; at room temperature, differences of about 1 kcal/mol already matter.

Force fields

Springs for bonds and angles, simple terms for torsions and contacts, with fitted parameters. Very fast.

UFF covers almost the whole periodic table; MMFF94 is more accurate for organic, drug-like molecules.

ANI neural potentials

Neural networks trained on millions of quantum-chemistry calculations. Much more accurate than force fields, far cheaper than quantum chemistry. GLOMOS uses an ensemble of 8 networks.

ANI-1ccx (H, C, N, O) approaches coupled-cluster accuracy; ANI-2x adds S, F and Cl. Both are for neutral, closed-shell molecules.

Lesson 4 · 4 min · interactive

Conformers: one molecule, many shapes

Single bonds can twist. Each twisted arrangement of the same molecule is a conformer. Nothing is broken or re-bonded — it is the same compound, just a different shape — but shapes differ in energy, and the low-energy ones are the ones you find in nature.

Butane, looking down the central C–C bond
Populations from the Boltzmann distribution, p ∝ g·e−ΔE/RT, with one anti and two gauche minima. Energies are typical textbook values (anti 0, gauche ≈ 0.9, eclipsed ≈ 3.6, syn ≈ 4.5 kcal/mol).

Butane has one twistable bond and three valleys. A drug molecule may have 5–10 twistable bonds: with three valleys each, that is 310 ≈ 59 000 combinations — too many to try one by one. That is the job of GLOMOS.

Lesson 5 · 4 min · interactive

GLOMOS step by step

GLOMOS is a genetic algorithm: like evolution, it keeps the fittest shapes (lowest energy), mixes them, mutates them, and repeats. The parameter names in monospace are the boxes in the GLOMOS panel of the explorer.

Reading your results

                    

                        

                            
ColumnMeaning

                            
                                
ΔE (kcal/mol)Energy above the best conformer (which has 0). Below ~1 kcal/mol: competitive; above ~3: rarely populated at room temperature.

                                
Pop. 298 K (%)Share of molecules expected in each shape at 25 °C (Boltzmann). Indicative: zero-point and thermal corrections are not included.

                                
A, B, C (GHz)Rotational constants, from the moments of inertia of each shape. Microwave spectroscopy measures them, so they tell conformers apart experimentally.

Glossary

                    

                        
Conformer
A 3D shape of a molecule reachable by rotating single bonds.

                        
Dihedral (torsion) angle
The twist between two bonds seen along the bond that joins them.

                        
Stereocentre
An atom whose four different neighbours can be arranged in two mirror-image ways (R/S).

                        
E/Z
The two arrangements of substituents around a double bond (opposite / same side).

                        
Potential energy surface
Energy as a function of the atomic positions — the "landscape" of lesson 3.

                        
Force field
A fast, parameterised energy model (bonds as springs, etc.).

                        
ANI
Neural-network potentials trained on quantum-chemistry data.

                        
kcal/mol
Energy unit used in chemistry; RT at 25 °C ≈ 0.59 kcal/mol.

                        
Boltzmann population
Fraction of molecules expected in each conformer at a given temperature.

                        
Rotational constants
A, B, C: inverse moments of inertia, measured by microwave spectroscopy.

Frequently asked questions

                    

                        
Is ELAYA free?
Yes. It is free to use and open source.

                        
What happens to the molecules I submit?
Conversions are computed and returned; they are not stored. GLOMOS jobs keep their files only while the job runs and for about two hours after it finishes, then they are deleted automatically. For confidential structures, run ELAYA on your own computer (see below).

                        
Which molecules can I use?
Any valid SMILES for 3D conversion. GLOMOS with ANI needs neutral, closed-shell molecules made of H, C, N, O (ANI-1x, ANI-1ccx), plus S, F and Cl with ANI-2x. The explorer warns you when a molecule is outside that domain.

                        
Why does GLOMOS take minutes?
It relaxes hundreds of candidate shapes with a neural network, and the public server shares a small CPU. Use the Fast preset and the one-network Screening for exploration; the time estimate under the panel learns from your previous runs.

                        
Which ANI model should I choose?
ANI-1ccx for the best accuracy on H, C, N, O molecules; ANI-2x if your molecule contains S, F or Cl. ANI-1x is the original baseline.

                        
Can I reproduce a run?
Yes: set a fixed Random seed in the GLOMOS panel and keep the same parameters.

                        
Can I run it on my own computer?
Yes, with Docker: docker build -t elaya . then docker run -p 5000:5000 elaya, and open http://localhost:5000. On a normal laptop GLOMOS is several times faster than on the free server.
